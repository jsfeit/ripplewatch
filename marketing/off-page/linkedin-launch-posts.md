### Post 1 — the origin story (personal, low-pitch)

Every job I've had, I've tracked competitors the same way: a spreadsheet, a few saved Google searches, and a habit of checking a competitor's pricing page right before a big deal.

It worked. Barely. And it always meant finding out about the moves that actually mattered — a pricing cut, a key hire, a positioning shift — after they'd already cost me a deal or a customer, not before.

I built Ripplewatch because I got tired of that gap. It's competitive intelligence that doesn't just tell you what changed — it tells you whether it matters to *your* business specifically, scored against your own positioning and the real reasons you've won or lost deals.

Launching it properly this week. More soon.

### Post 2 — the actual product mechanism (more direct pitch)

Most competitive intelligence tools have solved the same first problem: watch pricing pages, job boards, and news, then alert you when something changes.

The problem nobody solves is the next one — does this actually matter?

A pricing cut can be genuinely dangerous for one company and completely irrelevant for another, depending on their ICP and what's actually driving their lost deals. A generic "High/Medium/Low" severity score can't tell the difference. It's the same rating for every company reading it.

That's the gap Ripplewatch is built to close. You tell it your positioning, your ICP, and your real lost-deal/churn reasons once. Every signal after that gets scored against *that* context, not a generic scale.

Self-serve, published pricing, no sales call required to see it working: ripplewatch.ai

### Post 4 — a specific insight/data point (evergreen, reusable pattern)

One pattern that keeps showing up building Ripplewatch: teams don't actually need to watch *more* competitor signals. They need fewer, better-explained ones.

The tools we compared ourselves against in our own research (wrote it up here: [link to /state-of-competitive-intelligence]) mostly compete on breadth — more sources, more competitors, more alerts. Almost none of them compete on relevance: telling you which of those alerts is worth 5 minutes of your team's time today.

That's the bet behind Ripplewatch. Curious if others building in this space see it differently.
