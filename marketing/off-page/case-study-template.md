# Case study template

Scaffold for the first real customer case studies once there are customers willing to be featured. Do not fill this in with placeholder/hypothetical numbers and publish it — an empty scaffold is honest; a fabricated one isn't. Use this structure once real customer input exists.

---

## Working title format
"[Company] uses Ripplewatch to [specific outcome]" — e.g. "How [Company] caught a competitor's pricing cut before it cost them a renewal"

## Required inputs before writing (interview the customer for these)
- [ ] Company name, logo (with permission to use), industry, rough team size
- [ ] What they used before Ripplewatch (spreadsheet? nothing? a specific competitor tool named in /compare?) — real specifics, not "manual processes"
- [ ] The specific moment or signal that mattered (a real example, with their permission to reference even loosely — "a competitor's pricing change" is fine if they don't want the competitor named)
- [ ] What they did as a result (the action taken, not just "they were informed")
- [ ] A real, attributable quote — get 2-3 options from the interview, pick the most specific one, avoid generic praise ("Ripplewatch is great!" is not usable; "We caught [X] three days before it would have cost us the [Y] deal" is)
- [ ] Which tier they're on and roughly how many competitors they track (only if they're comfortable sharing — don't imply usage numbers that aren't confirmed)

## Structure

**1. The setup (2-3 sentences)**
Who the company is, what they do, and what their competitive landscape looks like before Ripplewatch.

**2. The problem (1 short paragraph)**
The specific gap — not "they needed competitive intelligence" but the actual failure mode: what they missed, when, and what it cost (a deal, a renewal, a positioning miss).

**3. What changed (1-2 paragraphs)**
How they use Ripplewatch specifically — which signals matter most to them, how their team actually incorporates alerts into their workflow (Slack? a weekly review? tied to deal reviews?).

**4. The result (concrete, specific)**
A real before/after, ideally with a number if the customer is comfortable sharing one (time saved, a specific deal saved, faster reaction time). If no hard number is available, a specific behavioral change is still legitimate ("the team now reviews competitor signals every Monday instead of finding out reactively").

**5. Pull quote**
The single best quote from the interview, attributed with name + title + company.

**6. CTA**
Link to /pricing and the specific /compare or /alternatives page relevant to what they switched from, if applicable.

## Format notes
- Target 400-600 words — case studies that run long lose readers before the pull quote
- Publish as its own blog post type (reuse the existing blog_posts table/renderer — no new content type needed) with a distinct visual treatment (customer logo near the top) if there's ever more than 2-3 of these
- Get written sign-off from the customer on the final draft before publishing, including the exact quote wording
