## Body

If you're comparing Crayon and Klue, you're probably past "should we track competitors" and into "which platform runs our compete program." That's a real decision, so it's worth being clear about what each one is, and when neither is the right size.

A note up front: we make Ripplewatch, which is a much smaller thing than either of these. We've described Crayon and Klue only from what they say on their own sites, checked on October 14, 2026, and we say where that leaves us unsure.

## What each one says it is

Crayon calls itself a competitive intelligence platform that monitors your competitors and enables your sales team. Its homepage lists competitor monitoring (intel in your inbox, AI summaries of news, importance scoring), content creation (battlecards, announcements, newsletters), sales enablement inside tools like Salesforce, Slack and Highspot, and performance metrics such as win/loss analysis and battlecard engagement.

Klue calls itself the AI platform for competitive intelligence and win-loss. It splits into two products: Compete Agent, described as an AI research analyst and deal assistant, and a Win-Loss suite built around buyer interviews, some run by Klue's analysts and some by an AI interviewer.

Both have moved toward AI assistants. Crayon announced an MCP server in September 2025 so its content can be used from tools like ChatGPT, Glean and Copilot. Klue's homepage shows use cases for asking questions and updating battlecards from an assistant such as Claude, plus an integration with Glean.

## Where they differ, going by their own pages

- Crayon leans on monitoring, and on getting battlecards in front of sellers inside the tools they already use.
- Klue leans on pairing competitive intelligence with win-loss research, including buyer interviews.
- Both look built for a company with someone running the program. Both homepages lead with a demo, and neither lists prices there.

If you have a competitive intelligence or product marketing owner and sellers who will open battlecards, either could fit, and seeing both demos is the only real way to choose. If your biggest gap is hearing buyers explain why they lost, Klue's win-loss side is what its pages emphasize most. If your gap is making sure reps see competitor changes fast, Crayon's pitch is closer to that.

## When neither is the right size

A lot of people reading comparisons like this are on teams of five to fifty with nobody in a CI role. A platform built around a program owner and a sales team mostly asks for something you don't have yet, which is a person to curate it. Smaller teams often get more from something lighter that answers a question when they ask it.

That's the gap Ripplewatch is aimed at. It's $29 a month plus usage you prepay for, and it works inside Claude or ChatGPT: you ask what changed with a competitor and whether it matters for your deals, and it answers from their pricing, hiring and press, scored against how you position yourself and the deals you've won and lost. It doesn't build battlecards or run buyer interviews, and it isn't a replacement for a platform built for a large sales team.

## A quick way to decide

- Does someone's job include competitive intelligence? If not, start light.
- Will your sellers actually use battlecards inside Salesforce or Slack? If yes, look at Crayon and Klue.
- Is your biggest unknown why you lose deals? Look hard at win-loss with whatever tool you pick. Even a handful of logged deals changes what you notice.
